# 📚 Day 5 — Literature Review

Part of the Machine Learning Research Roadmap series.

> Good researchers do not start from scratch. They learn from existing research first.

---

In Day 4, we learned how to create hypotheses from observations.

Before testing a hypothesis, researchers need to understand what is already known about the problem.

This process is called a literature review.

A literature review helps researchers:

- Understand existing knowledge
- Avoid repeating previous work
- Identify limitations in current research
- Discover new research opportunities

In this chapter, we'll learn how to conduct an effective literature review and use it to guide future research.

---

# 🤔 Why Do We Need Literature Reviews?

Imagine you want to study the Titanic dataset.

You create a research question and a hypothesis.

Before conducting experiments, an important question remains:

```text
Has someone already investigated this problem?
```

Researchers answer this question through a literature review.

---

## What Can We Learn from Previous Research?

A literature review helps us understand:

- What has already been discovered
- Which methods were successful
- Which methods failed
- What limitations exist
- What questions remain unanswered

---

## Example

Research Question:

```text
Does passenger age influence survival probability?
```

Before investigating this question ourselves, we should examine existing studies to see:

- Whether the relationship has already been studied
- What conclusions were reached
- Whether gaps still exist

---

### Key Idea

Research does not begin with experiments.

Research begins with understanding existing knowledge.

---

# 📖 What is a Literature Review?

A literature review is a systematic examination of existing research related to a topic.

Its purpose is not simply to read papers.

Its purpose is to understand the current state of knowledge.

---

## Literature Review vs Reading Papers

| Reading Papers | Literature Review |
|---------------|------------------|
| Individual activity | Structured process |
| Focuses on one paper | Combines findings from many papers |
| Understands a study | Understands an entire research area |

---

### Goal

A literature review answers:

```text
What is already known?

What is not yet known?
```

# 📄 What Should We Look For in a Research Paper?

Beginners often try to read every line of a paper.

Researchers focus on specific sections.

---

## 1. Research Problem

What problem is being solved?

---

## 2. Dataset

What data was used?

---

## 3. Methodology

What approach was used?

---

## 4. Results

What were the findings?

---

## 5. Limitations

What problems remain unsolved?

---

### Researcher's Checklist

When reading a paper, ask:

- What was studied?
- How was it studied?
- What was discovered?
- What limitations exist?
- What future work was suggested?

---

# 📋 How to Conduct a Literature Review

A literature review is a structured process.

The goal is to move from a broad topic to a deep understanding of existing research.

---

## Step 1 — Define a Research Topic

Example:

```text
Titanic Survival Prediction
```

---

## Step 2 — Identify Keywords

Example:

```text
Titanic
Machine Learning
Survival Prediction
Feature Selection
Classification
```

---

## Step 3 — Search for Literature

Use:

- Google Scholar
- arXiv
- Semantic Scholar
- ResearchGate

---

## Step 4 — Select Relevant Papers

Focus on papers that are:

- Related to your topic
- Recent when possible
- Frequently cited
- Methodologically sound

---

## Step 5 — Extract Key Information

Record:

- Research Problem
- Dataset
- Methodology
- Results
- Limitations

---

## Step 6 — Compare Findings

Look for:

- Common conclusions
- Contradictory findings
- Research trends

---

## Step 7 — Identify Gaps

Ask:

```text
What has not been studied yet?
```

This is where new research opportunities emerge.

---

# 📊 Literature Review Matrix

Researchers often summarize papers using a literature review matrix.

A matrix helps compare studies efficiently.

---

## Example Matrix

| Paper | Research Problem | Dataset | Method | Key Findings | Limitations |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| Paper A | Titanic Survival Prediction | Titanic | Logistic Regression | Good baseline performance | Limited feature engineering |
| Paper B | Survival Prediction | Titanic | Random Forest | Improved accuracy | Higher complexity |
| Paper C | Passenger Analysis | Titanic | Decision Tree | Easy interpretation | Lower predictive performance |

---

### Benefits

A literature review matrix helps researchers:

- Organize information
- Compare studies
- Identify trends
- Discover research gaps
- Avoid repeatedly reading the same papers

---

# 📝 Example Literature Review

Suppose we are interested in:

```text
Titanic Survival Prediction
```

After reviewing several studies, we might discover:

- Logistic Regression is frequently used as a baseline model.
- Tree-based methods often achieve higher predictive performance.
- Feature engineering significantly affects results.
- Most studies focus on prediction rather than explainability.

---

### Possible Research Gap

Many studies focus on improving accuracy.

Fewer studies investigate model interpretability and explainability.

This observation could inspire a new research question.

---

# ⚠️ Common Beginner Mistakes

Many beginners struggle with literature reviews.

---

## ❌ Reading Only One Paper

Research should be based on multiple sources rather than a single study.

---

## ❌ Focusing Only on Results

Researchers should also examine:

- Methodology
- Dataset
- Assumptions
- Limitations

---

## ❌ Ignoring Limitations

Limitations often reveal opportunities for future research.

---

## ❌ Collecting Papers Without Organizing Them

A literature review matrix helps organize findings and compare studies.

---

### Research Tip

Do not ask:

```text
What does this paper say?
```

Ask:

```text
What can I learn from this paper?
```


# 🎯 Day 3 — Research Question Formulation

Part of the Machine Learning Research Roadmap series.

> Good research begins with a good question.

---

Most beginners start with:

"I want to build a model."

Researchers start with:

"What exactly am I trying to understand?"

A well-formulated research question provides direction, defines scope, and determines how a study will be conducted.

In this chapter, we'll learn how to transform vague ideas into clear, researchable questions.

---

# 🤔 Topic vs Research Question

Many beginners confuse a topic with a research question.

A topic is a broad area of interest.

A research question is a specific problem that can be investigated.

---

| Topic | Research Question |
|---------|---------|
| Titanic Dataset | Does feature selection improve Titanic survival prediction performance? |
| Healthcare | Can machine learning predict diabetes risk using demographic data? |
| Education | Does student attendance influence academic performance? |
| Finance | Can transaction patterns improve fraud detection accuracy? |

---

### Why This Matters

A topic tells us **where to look**.

A research question tells us **what to investigate**.

Without a clear research question, research often becomes random experimentation.

---

### Think About It

Topic:

```text
Titanic Dataset
```

Research Question:

```text
Does feature selection improve Titanic survival prediction performance?
```

Notice how the second statement can be tested, measured, and evaluated.

---
# ✅ Characteristics of a Good Research Question

Not all questions make good research questions.

Strong research questions have the following characteristics:

| Characteristic | Description |
|---------------|-------------|
| Specific | Clearly defines what is being investigated |
| Measurable | Can be evaluated using data |
| Researchable | Can be tested through experiments or analysis |
| Relevant | Solves an important problem |
| Focused | Has a clear scope |

---

## Example of a Weak Question

```text
Can machine learning help healthcare?
```

Problems:

- Too broad
- No clear objective
- Difficult to evaluate

---

## Example of a Strong Question

```text
Can machine learning predict diabetes risk using demographic and lifestyle data?
```

Why it is better:

- Specific problem
- Clear outcome
- Measurable
- Researchable

---

### Quick Rule

A good research question should tell us:

1. What is being studied?
2. What is being measured?
3. What data will be used?

# 🔄 Transforming Ideas into Research Questions

Most research projects begin with a simple idea.

The challenge is transforming that idea into a researchable question.

---

## Example 1 — Titanic Dataset

### Initial Idea

```text
I want to predict Titanic survival.
```

### Better Question

```text
Which passenger attributes contribute most to survival prediction?
```

### Research Question

```text
Does feature selection improve Titanic survival prediction performance?
```

---

## Example 2 — Healthcare

### Initial Idea

```text
I want to use machine learning in healthcare.
```

### Better Question

```text
Can machine learning identify high-risk patients?
```

### Research Question

```text
Can machine learning predict diabetes risk using demographic and lifestyle data?
```

---

## Example 3 — Education

### Initial Idea

```text
I want to analyze student performance.
```

### Better Question

```text
Which factors affect academic performance?
```

### Research Question

```text
Does student attendance significantly influence academic performance?
```

---

## Example 4 — Finance

### Initial Idea

```text
I want to detect fraud.
```

### Better Question

```text
Can transaction patterns identify fraudulent activity?
```

### Research Question

```text
Can transaction-based features improve fraud detection accuracy?
```

---

### Key Insight

Researchers rarely start with perfect questions.

They refine broad ideas into specific, measurable, and testable questions.

# ⚠️ Common Beginner Mistakes

Many beginners struggle with research question formulation.

Here are some common mistakes:

---

## ❌ Questions That Are Too Broad

Example:

```text
Can AI improve healthcare?
```

Problem:

- Huge scope
- Difficult to evaluate
- No clear objective

---

## ❌ Questions That Cannot Be Measured

Example:

```text
Is machine learning interesting?
```

Problem:

- Subjective
- No measurable outcome

---

## ❌ Questions Without Data

Example:

```text
Can we predict future world events?
```

Problem:

- No clear dataset
- Difficult to validate

---

## ❌ Questions Without a Clear Outcome

Example:

```text
Can machine learning be used in education?
```

Problem:

- Too vague
- No specific target

---

### Better Approach

Ask questions that are:

- Specific
- Measurable
- Researchable
- Relevant
- Focused

## Self-Assessment Checklist

Before accepting your question, ask:

- Is it specific?
- Is it measurable?
- Is it researchable?
- Is it relevant?
- Is it focused?

If the answer is yes to all five, you likely have a strong research question.

# ✅ Key Takeaways

- A topic is not the same as a research question.
- Good research questions are specific, measurable, researchable, relevant, and focused.
- Strong questions guide the entire research process.
- Broad ideas should be refined into testable questions.
- Research begins with asking the right question.

---

### Remember

> Better questions lead to better research.

> Weak questions lead to weak conclusions.
